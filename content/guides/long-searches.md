# When the search is taking a long time

Searches often take months, not weeks. A long search isn't a sign you're doing it wrong, but
it is a reason to check a few things and to look after yourself.

## Check the numbers, not your worth

Out of 100 people who apply for a typical role, about 1 gets the offer
([How hiring works](/how-hiring-works)). After a few months, look at your tracker:

| What you see | What it usually means | What to try |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Few or no first calls | The CV or the roles aren't matching at the screening step | Run the CV check against the ads you're applying to; aim at roles you meet most requirements for |
| First calls but no interviews | The phone screen is where it stops | Practise your two-minute summary and your salary answer |
| Interviews but no offers | You're close, and it's competitive | Ask for feedback; practise stories for the questions that went least well |

## Keep a pace you can keep

- Set a weekly number of good applications, not a daily target you'll break.
- Take a day off from it each week. Searching every day does not make replies arrive faster.
- Count the steps you control (applications sent, people contacted, practice done), not only
  replies.

## Look after yourself

If the search is affecting your sleep, mood or health, that comes first. Your GP is a good
place to start. Talking to someone you trust about how it's going, not only about leads,
helps more than people expect.

## If you've been made redundant

**Ireland.** If you have at least 104 weeks' continuous service, statutory redundancy is two
weeks' gross pay per year of service plus one extra week, with weekly pay capped at €600. The
statutory payment is tax-free. Many employers pay more. ([Fórsa summary](https://www.forsa.ie/other-benefits/workplace-rights/redundancy/);
check [Citizens Information](https://www.citizensinformation.ie) for your case.)

**UK.** With at least two years' service: half a week's pay per year under age 22, one week per
year from 22 to 40, and one and a half weeks per year from 41, counting up to 20 years. From
6 April 2026, weekly pay is capped at £751 in Great Britain and £783 in Northern Ireland
([CIPP](https://www.cipp.org.uk/resources/news/statutory-redundancy-rates-announced-for-2026-27.html)).
Check [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/redundant-your-rights) or [nidirect](https://www.nidirect.gov.uk) for your case.

Also check: your notice pay, holiday pay owed, and whether you can register for jobseeker
support straight away. A redundancy is about the role, not you, and you can say so plainly
in interviews: "My role was made redundant in a restructure."
