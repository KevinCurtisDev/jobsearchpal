# Ask someone you know

Most of Hearback helps with cold applications. This guide is about the other door in.

## Why it matters

In one large study of small employers, referrals were about 2 in every 100 applicants but
about 11 in every 100 hires ([CareerPlug data via StaffingHub, 2024, 60,000+ small businesses,
mostly US](https://staffinghub.com/hiring/company-pages-referrals-result-in-more-hires-recruiting-metrics-report/)).
A referral doesn't get you the job. It gets a person to read your CV, which is the step most
applications never reach.

## Who counts as "someone you know"

Not only close friends. Former colleagues, people from your course, a friend's partner,
someone you met at an event, a person who left the company last year. Most referrals come
from people you know a little, not a lot.

## How to ask without it feeling awkward

1. **Pick a specific role.** "Do you know of anything?" is hard to answer. "I'm applying for
   the analyst role in your team" is easy.
2. **Make it easy to say no.** People are more willing when they're not cornered.
3. **Send what they'd need:** the job link, your CV, and two lines on why you fit.

> Hi [name], I hope you're well. I noticed [company] is hiring for [role] and I'm planning to
> apply. Would you be comfortable referring me, or telling me a bit about the team? Completely
> fine if not. I've attached my CV and the link, and the short version is: [two lines on fit].

## If you don't know anyone there

Ask a second-degree contact for a 15-minute chat about the team, not a referral. If it goes
well, they may offer. If they don't, you still learn things that make your application better.

## In Hearback

Each tracked application has a **"Who do I know there?"** prompt where you can mark *not yet*,
*asked* or *referred*. Hearback doesn't ask for or store the other person's name, and nothing
is sent from Hearback: you always contact people yourself. Later, "What works for you"
compares how referred and cold applications did, once you have enough of each.
