# Searching from a different starting point

Short, practical notes for situations the general advice doesn't fit.

## Coming back after a gap

- Put the gap on your CV in one plain line: "Career break: caring for a family member" or
  "Career break: travel and study". Explanations longer than a line draw attention.
- Lead with what's current: recent courses, volunteering, freelance work, anything you've kept up.
- Look for **returner programmes**: paid placements for people coming back after a break,
  run by some larger employers in Ireland and the UK.

## Changing career

- Your CV should open with the job you want, not the one you had: a two-line summary that
  connects the two.
- Translate your experience into the new field's words. Hearback's keyword check shows which
  of the ad's skills you already have under a different name.
- Expect a lower first-call rate for a while. Referrals and short conversations with people
  in the field (see "Ask someone you know") matter even more here.

## Graduates and first jobs

- Graduate schemes often open almost a year before the start date. Check closing dates early.
- Projects, part-time work and societies all count as experience. Write them like jobs: what
  you did and what came of it.

## Disability, health conditions and adjustments

- **You don't have to tell an employer** about a disability or health condition unless you
  want to. It's your choice when and whether to share.
- **You can ask for adjustments to the process,** such as extra time, a different format, or
  a quieter room, without giving a diagnosis.
- **UK:** under the Equality Act 2010, employers generally can't ask about health before a job
  offer, except for limited reasons such as arranging adjustments for the interview.
  **Ireland:** under the Employment Equality Acts, employers must take reasonable steps
  ("appropriate measures") so that people with disabilities can apply and work.
- Hearback never asks about health or disability outside the opt-in fairness audit, and
  never uses it in matching.

This guide is general information, not legal advice.
